PCOS Health Tip of the Day

Low-GI Eating for PCOS: About 70–80% of women with PCOS have insulin resistance. Switching to a low glycaemic index (GI) diet — whole grains, legumes, vegetables, and lean proteins — can significantly reduce insulin levels and improve hormonal balance. Even a 5–10% reduction in body weight can restore menstrual regularity and reduce androgen levels.

Source: 2023 International PCOS Guideline (Teede et al.)

Understanding Your PCOS Symptoms
Fatigue & Low Energy

Often caused by insulin resistance and disrupted sleep. Regular light exercise and balanced blood sugar through low-GI eating can significantly improve energy levels.

Mood Swings & Anxiety

Women with PCOS are 3x more likely to experience anxiety and depression. Hormonal fluctuations directly affect neurotransmitters. Mindfulness, yoga, and consistent sleep can help.

Acne & Skin Issues

Elevated androgens stimulate sebaceous glands, causing persistent acne especially on the jawline and back. Anti-androgen medications and a low-GI diet can reduce breakouts.

Exercise Tips for PCOS
🏃 Aerobic Exercise

Aim for 150 min/week of moderate aerobic activity. Walking, swimming, and cycling are excellent for improving insulin sensitivity without stressing joints.

💪 Strength Training

2–3 sessions/week builds lean muscle mass, which improves glucose metabolism. Start with bodyweight exercises, then progress to weights.

🧘 Yoga & Mindfulness

Regular yoga reduces cortisol and insulin levels. Research shows yoga specifically benefits PCOS patients by reducing anxiety and hormonal imbalance.

Great question! For PCOS management, limit: 🚫 Refined carbs (white bread, white rice, pastries), 🚫 Sugary drinks & fruit juices, 🚫 Processed snacks, 🚫 High-GI foods like potatoes. Metformin is commonly prescribed for PCOS, especially when insulin resistance is present. It works by reducing insulin levels, which in turn reduces androgen production.
