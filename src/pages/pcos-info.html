Understanding PCOS

Comprehensive, evidence-based information to help you understand, manage, and thrive with Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common endocrine disorder affecting women of reproductive age. Despite its name, PCOS involves much more than just ovarian cysts—it's a complex condition involving hormonal imbalance, insulin resistance, and inflammation.

PCOS affects approximately 8–13% of women worldwide, making it one of the most common reproductive disorders. In developing countries like Sri Lanka, the prevalence is around 13%, affecting hundreds of thousands of women across the country.

The condition is characterized by three diagnostic criteria (Rotterdam Criteria), and a diagnosis requires at least 2 of the 3:

  • 1. Ovulatory dysfunction: Irregular or absent menstrual cycles (fewer than 8 cycles/year)
  • 2. Hyperandrogenism (excess androgens): Clinical signs (hirsutism, acne) or elevated testosterone levels
  • 3. Polycystic ovaries: Multiple small follicles visible on ultrasound (≥12 per ovary)

Symptoms of PCOS

PCOS symptoms vary widely between women and can change over time. Here are the most common manifestations:

🩸

Irregular Periods

Periods that are infrequent, unpredictable, or absent. Cycles may be longer than 35 days or fewer than 8 cycles per year. Some women experience prolonged bleeding.

🌱

Excess Hair Growth (Hirsutism)

Unwanted facial and body hair due to elevated androgen levels. This commonly affects the face, chest, back, and stomach areas.

🧴

Acne & Oily Skin

Hormonal acne that often appears on the face, chest, and back due to increased sebaceous gland activity from elevated androgens.

💇

Hair Loss

Hair thinning or male-pattern baldness (androgenetic alopecia) on the scalp, often while facial/body hair increases paradoxically.

⚖️

Weight Gain & Difficulty Losing Weight

Unexplained weight gain, especially in the abdomen. Insulin resistance makes it harder to lose weight despite diet and exercise.

😴

Fatigue & Sleep Issues

Persistent tiredness, low energy, and sleep disturbances. Often related to insulin resistance, hormonal imbalances, and sometimes sleep apnea.

🧠

Mood Disturbances

Depression, anxiety, and mood swings are 2-3 times more common in women with PCOS. Related to hormonal fluctuations and emotional stress.

🚫

Infertility or Subfertility

Difficulty getting pregnant due to irregular ovulation. PCOS is one of the leading causes of infertility, but many treatments can help.

🩹

Skin Darkening (Acanthosis Nigricans)

Dark, velvety patches of skin, usually in body creases (neck, armpits, groin). A sign of insulin resistance.

Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of PCOS is not fully understood, but research suggests a combination of genetic and environmental factors:

🧬 Genetic Factors

  • Family History: PCOS runs in families. If your mother or sister has PCOS, your risk is significantly higher.
  • Multiple Genes Involved: This is not a single-gene condition. Many genes contribute to PCOS susceptibility.

🔬 Insulin Resistance

  • Most Common Factor: 50-70% of women with PCOS have insulin resistance. Cells don't respond properly to insulin.
  • Cascade Effect: This causes the pancreas to produce excess insulin, which triggers excess androgen production, disrupting ovulation.

⚡ Inflammation

  • Chronic Low-Grade Inflammation: Women with PCOS have elevated inflammatory markers.
  • Stimulates Androgens: This inflammation may trigger polycystic ovaries to produce excess male hormones.

🌿 Environmental & Lifestyle

  • Diet & Weight: High-sugar, low-fiber diets increase PCOS risk and severity.
  • Sedentary Lifestyle: Lack of physical activity contributes to insulin resistance and weight gain.

Diagnosis of PCOS

There is no single definitive test for PCOS. Diagnosis is based on clinical features and test results meeting the Rotterdam Criteria, which requires at least 2 of 3 features:

Diagnostic Tests Include:

  • ✓ Blood Tests: Testosterone, FSH, LH, glucose, insulin levels, lipid profile
  • ✓ Pelvic Ultrasound: To visualize ovaries for multiple follicles
  • ✓ Pelvic Examination: To check for signs of excess androgen
  • ✓ Medical History: Irregular periods, infertility, family history
  • ✓ Other Tests: Glucose tolerance test, thyroid function, to rule out other conditions

Treatment & Management

PCOS is manageable with a combination of medical and lifestyle treatments tailored to your individual needs:

💊

Hormonal Contraceptives

Regulate menstrual cycles, reduce androgen levels, improve acne and hair growth. Usually first-line treatment.

🩺

Metformin

Improves insulin resistance, helps regulate cycles, supports weight management, and reduces diabetes risk.

🌱

Lifestyle Modifications

Even 5-10% weight loss significantly improves PCOS. Regular exercise, balanced diet, stress management crucial.

🧴

Anti-Androgen Medications

Spironolactone, flutamide reduce effects of excess androgens. Help with acne, hair loss, and hirsutism.

🍽️

Nutritional Management

Low glycaemic index diet, anti-inflammatory foods (omega-3s, leafy greens), adequate protein. Avoid refined sugars.

🚼

Fertility Treatments

For women trying to conceive: ovulation induction medications, hormone therapies, and assisted reproductive techniques.

PCOS & Long-Term Health Risks

🩺 Type 2 Diabetes

Women with PCOS have significantly higher risk of developing type 2 diabetes (up to 40% risk). Regular monitoring and lifestyle changes are important.

❤️ Cardiovascular Disease

Elevated cholesterol, blood pressure, and inflammatory markers increase heart disease risk. Regular cardiac screening recommended.

📊 Metabolic Syndrome

A cluster of conditions including obesity, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, and insulin resistance—common in PCOS.

🧠 Mental Health Issues

Depression (2-3x higher) and anxiety are common. Body image concerns, infertility worries, and hormonal changes all contribute.

🏥 Endometrial Hyperplasia

Infrequent periods increase endometrial cancer risk. Regular monitoring and treatments to induce periods are recommended.

🤰 Pregnancy Complications

Higher risks of gestational diabetes, preeclampsia, and miscarriage. With proper management, successful pregnancies are very achievable.

Lifestyle Management Strategies

🥗 Nutrition

  • Choose Low Glycaemic Index Foods: Whole grains, legumes, vegetables, fruits. Avoid white rice, refined flour, sugary foods.
  • Anti-Inflammatory Diet: Fatty fish (salmon, mackerel), leafy greens, berries, nuts, olive oil. Omega-3 fatty acids help.
  • Adequate Protein: Helps with satiety and blood sugar control. Aim for lean meat, fish, tofu, legumes.
  • Limit Inflammatory Foods: Reduce processed foods, saturated fats, sugary drinks, and fried foods.
  • Stay Hydrated: Drink plenty of water. Aim for 8+ glasses daily.

🏃 Exercise

  • Aim for 150+ Minutes Weekly: Moderate-intensity aerobic activity like brisk walking, cycling, swimming.
  • Strength Training: 2-3 days per week improves insulin sensitivity and helps with weight management.
  • High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT): Short bursts of intense exercise can be very effective for PCOS.
  • Find Activities You Enjoy: You're more likely to stick with it long-term.

😴 Sleep & Stress

  • Aim for 7-9 Hours Nightly: Poor sleep worsens insulin resistance and hormonal balance. Maintain consistent sleep schedule.
  • Manage Stress: Cortisol affects hormones and inflammation. Try: yoga, meditation, deep breathing, journaling.
  • Limit Screen Time Before Bed: Blue light interferes with melatonin production.
  • Create Sleep-Friendly Environment: Dark, cool, quiet bedroom. Avoid caffeine late in the day.

🧠 Mental Health

  • Seek Support: Talk to friends, family, or support groups. You're not alone in this journey.
  • Consider Therapy: Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) has evidence for improving depression and anxiety in PCOS.
  • Practice Self-Compassion: PCOS is challenging, but you're managing it well. Celebrate small wins.
  • Body Acceptance: Work on accepting your body and focusing on health rather than appearance.

Living Well with PCOS

PCOS is a chronic condition, but it's highly manageable. With the right combination of medical care, lifestyle changes, and support, you can control symptoms and live a healthy, fulfilling life.

Remember: Every person's PCOS is different. Work with your healthcare team to develop a personalized management plan. Track your symptoms, learn what works for your body, and stay committed to your health journey.
