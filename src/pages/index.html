About PCOS Care Hub

Bridging the Gap in PCOS Healthcare in Sri Lanka

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a chronic hormonal condition affecting 8–13% of women of reproductive age. In Sri Lanka, many women struggle with fragmented paper-based records, limited doctor-patient communication, and lack of a centralized management system.

PCOS Care Hub was built specifically to address these gaps — offering a secure, web-based platform where patients, hospitals, and administrators all work together in one unified ecosystem.

Patient Portal: Track symptoms, menstrual cycles, lifestyle data, upload reports and view your health timeline.
Hospital Interface: Manage lab results, consultations, and patient records under controlled, role-based access.
Admin Dashboard: Full system oversight, hospital approvals, user management and audit logs.

PCOS Knowledge Center

Everything You Need to Know About PCOS

Evidence-based information to help you understand, manage, and thrive with PCOS.

🩸

Irregular Periods

Having fewer than 8 menstrual cycles per year, cycles longer than 35 days, or complete absence of menstruation. This is one of the most common PCOS symptoms.

🌱

Excess Androgen

Elevated male hormones causing excess facial/body hair (hirsutism), severe acne, and male-pattern baldness or thinning hair on the scalp.

🔬

Polycystic Ovaries

Enlarged ovaries containing many small fluid-filled sacs (follicles) that surround the eggs, visible on ultrasound. Despite the name, cysts may not always be present.

⚖️

Weight Changes

Unexplained weight gain, especially around the abdomen, or difficulty losing weight despite diet and exercise. Insulin resistance is often a contributing factor.

😴

Fatigue & Sleep Issues

Persistent tiredness, low energy, and difficulty sleeping are common in PCOS, often linked to hormonal imbalances and insulin resistance.

🧠

Mood Changes

Higher rates of depression, anxiety, and mood swings are associated with PCOS due to hormonal fluctuations and the emotional impact of chronic illness.

🧬

Insulin Resistance

Up to 70% of women with PCOS have insulin resistance. When cells don't respond properly to insulin, the pancreas produces more, which can stimulate excess androgen production.

🔬

Genetics

PCOS tends to run in families. If your mother or sister has PCOS, you have a significantly higher risk. Multiple genes are likely involved, making it a complex genetic condition.

⚡

Hormonal Imbalance

Excess production of androgens (male hormones) disrupts follicle development, preventing regular ovulation. LH and FSH imbalances also contribute to cycle irregularities.

🌿

Inflammation

Low-grade chronic inflammation is common in PCOS and may stimulate polycystic ovaries to produce androgens. Diet, lifestyle, and gut health all play roles.

💊

Hormonal Contraceptives

Combined oral contraceptives help regulate menstrual cycles, reduce androgen levels, clear acne, and decrease hair growth. Often first-line treatment for cycle irregularities.

🩺

Metformin

An insulin-sensitizing medication that can improve insulin resistance, help regulate menstrual cycles, support weight management, and reduce long-term diabetes risk.

🌱

Lifestyle Therapy

Even 5–10% weight loss can significantly improve PCOS symptoms, restore ovulation, and reduce health risks. Regular exercise and a balanced diet are foundational treatments.

🔬

Fertility Treatments

For women trying to conceive, options include ovulation induction (Clomifene, Letrozole), Gonadotropins, and in some cases, laparoscopic ovarian drilling.

🥗

Low Glycaemic Diet

Foods that don't spike blood sugar — whole grains, legumes, vegetables, and fruits — help manage insulin resistance. Avoid white rice, refined flour, and sugary drinks.

🐟

Anti-Inflammatory Foods

Include fatty fish (salmon, mackerel), leafy greens, berries, nuts, and olive oil. Omega-3 fatty acids can reduce androgen levels and improve insulin sensitivity.

🏃

Regular Exercise

Aim for 150+ minutes of moderate activity weekly. Strength training improves insulin sensitivity; cardio helps with weight management. Even brisk walking makes a difference.

😴

Sleep & Stress Management

Poor sleep worsens insulin resistance and hormonal balance. Aim for 7–9 hours. Stress-reduction techniques like yoga, meditation, and mindfulness can help regulate cortisol.

💙

Depression & Anxiety

Women with PCOS are significantly more likely to experience depression and anxiety. Hormonal fluctuations, body image concerns, and fertility worries all contribute. Seeking support is vital.

🧘

Body Image & Self-Esteem

Visible symptoms like acne, hair changes, and weight gain can affect self-confidence. PCOS Care Hub connects you with supportive communities and resources to build resilience.

👥

Support Networks

Connecting with others who have PCOS reduces isolation and improves mental wellbeing. Online communities, local support groups, and therapy can all make a meaningful difference.

🌟

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy

CBT has shown evidence for improving depression, anxiety, and body image in PCOS. Don't hesitate to speak with a mental health professional who understands chronic conditions.
