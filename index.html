About PCOS

Understanding Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

PCOS is the most common hormonal disorder in women of reproductive age — affecting 6–13% of women worldwide (WHO, 2023). It is a leading cause of infertility and is closely linked to insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular disease.

In Sri Lanka, limited digital health infrastructure and reliance on paper records makes PCOS management especially challenging for both patients and providers.

Hormonal ImbalanceElevated androgens disrupt the menstrual cycle. About 95% of PCOS patients have elevated insulin levels which drive androgen production.
Insulin ResistanceUp to 70% of PCOS patients have insulin resistance. A low-GI diet with regular exercise is the most effective first-line treatment.
Mental Health ImpactWomen with PCOS are 3× more likely to experience anxiety and depression. South Asian women face additional cultural barriers (Kumarapeli et al., 2011).
Long-term Health RisksUnmanaged PCOS raises the risk of type 2 diabetes by 5–10×, endometrial cancer, and cardiovascular disease. Regular monitoring reduces risk significantly.

💡 PCOS Management Tips
✅ Follow a low-GI diet — whole grains, legumes, vegetables
✅ Exercise at least 30 min daily — improves insulin sensitivity
✅ Track your cycle to detect irregularities early
✅ Reduce stress — practise yoga, meditation, or deep breathing
✅ Get regular blood glucose and hormone panel checks
✅ Maintain healthy weight — even 5% loss improves symptoms

Digital Health Gap

A 2025 JMIR study of PCOS apps found critical gaps in hospital integration and evidence-based content. Most apps focus only on personal self-management without connecting to clinical systems — the exact gap PCOS Care Hub addresses.

Arabkermani et al., J Med Internet Res, 2025
🏥

Hospital-Mediated Access

Research confirms continuity of care in chronic diseases like PCOS requires multi-stakeholder systems. Existing apps are predominantly consumer-facing tools without the hospital-patient interface needed for institutional healthcare settings.

Fahimeh Solat et al., BMC Women's Health, 2025
🌏

South Asian Context

Sri Lankan and South Asian women with PCOS face unique challenges: cultural stigma, disintegrated paper-based records, and limited health awareness. This platform is designed specifically for this local context, unlike Western-oriented international apps.

Kumarapeli et al., NIH, 2011
🤖

AI in PCOS Management

A 2025 Frontiers review found AI tools show significant promise in PCOS pattern detection and management. Intelligent assistants that provide personalised guidance improve patient adherence and self-management outcomes.

Wang et al., PMC, 2025
📊

Lifestyle as Treatment

The 2023 International PCOS Guideline strongly recommends lifestyle intervention as first-line treatment. Regular tracking of diet, exercise, and symptoms leads to measurable improvement — validating the lifestyle modules in this platform.

Teede et al., JCEM, 2023
💊

Insulin Resistance Focus

Approximately 95% of PCOS patients have elevated insulin levels. Digital platforms that educate patients on low-GI nutrition and track metabolic markers help address the root cause of PCOS symptoms rather than managing them superficially.

Lilli Health / MedCity News, 2025
